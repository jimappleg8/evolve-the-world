# Overview of the ideas behind this effort

This is me trying to explain my persepctive 🙂

I see a growing number of people who understand that the way the world currently works is not benefiting most people or the planet; they want humanity to move toward a better world. In 2000, Paul Ray and Sherry Anderson named this group the "Cultural Creatives" in their book by the same name.

People have different names for the process of building that world: creating Game B, the Great Turning, The Great Transition, moving toward a Regenerative Economy, Solar Punk and so on.

I think it's understood by most that this better world cannot be created from the top down. No one person or group has the wisdom to design this for everyone else, so it must emerge from the efforts of every day people. But I think a lot of people are unsure how they can participate in what we might call this Great Collaboration toward a better world.

So, I've been developing a loosely coupled system of ideas that might help. Here are some of the basic assertions I'm making:

- The most effective way to create positive change is to create alternatives to the systems that are causing so much damage to people and the planet. The goal is to make the old systems obsolete. I call these systems "artifacts" (influenced by Bucky Fuller) and they can literally be anything that humans create, from toys to governments.
- These alternative artifacts need to be developed collaboratively. No one person or group has the wisdom to design an artifact for all people. We need a diversity of people with a diversity of experience to co-design these artifacts. We also need a diversity of artifacts.
- The Open Source Software Movement offers a great model for global collaboration on projects. This model can be applied (and is already being applied) to projects far beyond just software. I call these **Open Artifacts** projects.
- The goal of an open artifacts project is to give an idea a life of it's own. If we never communicate our ideas or if we hide them away as intellectual property, they will die with us. By *creating* an open artifacts project to embody our idea, we communicate our idea, yes, but we also invite others to *contribute* their best experience, knowledge and skills to develop and improve that idea. As an open artifact, that idea can continue to grow and improve long after we've lost interest or moved on.
- To continue the metaphor of an open artifact being a living idea, the primary purpose of an open artifact is to reproduce itself. At it's core, an open artifact is an instruction manual on how to construct an artifact in the physical world, published on the internet, usually in the form of a website. Again, it can represent an object, an organization or anything humans can dream up.
- An open artifact's instructions should be flexible so that a person or group can *adapt* the artifact's design to their local needs. This allows the artifact to spread across the planet, scaling not by creating centralized, monolithic artifacts but by cultivating a large number of small, locally-responsive artifacts everywhere.
- Each open artifact is maintained independently, so there is no central authority managing or censoring them. Considered together, they represent a common resource (a commons) and a distributed platform for developing artifacts that move us toward a better world.
- All of this points to a new kind of role that we can choose to take on: the **Cultural Creator** role. A cultural creator is simply a person who chooses to help create a better world. Ideally, we would do that by *creating*, *contributing to* and *adapting* open artifacts. In that way, we can feel confident that our work is contributing to the commons and the common good.
- We can also form local groups of cultural creators with the goal of improving our local communities. These are critical because without people focused on adapting open artifacts, the commons is like a library that changes nothing. I call these groups **Evolutionary Design Groups**. They work together to determine what their community needs, and they either create or adapt open artifacts to fill those needs.
- As cultural creators, we engage in a **Creative Practice** which is personally tailored to our needs. It's a cyclical process where we 1) find work that needs doing, 2) choose what work is ours to do at this moment, 3) do the work, and 4) reflect on how the process is going. This process puts us in complete control of *what* we work on, *when* we work on it, and *how much* we contribute. It allows people from all walks of life to contribute, even when jobs and family demand most of our time. The short cycle allows us to pivot quickly depending on our passions and the skills we want to build.
- A creative practice allows us to improve the world while simultaneously improving ourselves, or what I call strengthening our **Personal Universe**. The best way to move toward being a more whole human being is to actively engage with others pursuing the same goal. When we create, contribute to and adapt open artifacts, we find ourselves constantly challenging our personal universe (or what we think we know about ourselves and others), and that brings growth.

The goal of EvolveTheWorld.org is to try to communicate these ideas, develop the infrastructure needed to make the whole thing viable, and to encourage people like you to embrace the role of Cultural Creator and help build this commons.
